Degrees or radians?
Let the student choose.

Every trig question gets a DEG | RAD switch. A few questions only make sense in one unit. Those lock the switch and say why, so the limit becomes a lesson.

The rule

Both (default)

Unit circle, exact values, identities, solving equations, inverse trig, laws of sines and cosines, polar coordinates. Answer 30° or π/6, both count.

Radians only

Graphs on a real x-axis, arc length s = rθ, sector area ½r²θ, angular speed, the radian itself, and all calculus.

Degrees only

Early geometry (Grades 4–8): protractors, angle sums, polygons. Bearings and navigation, roof pitch, real-world slopes.

Try it

Tip: type an answer with no unit, like 30 in RAD mode, to see the gentle "did you mean?" nudge.

Why radians?

The slope of sin x at zero tells the story. In radians it is exactly 1, so d/dx sin x = cos x. In degrees it is π/180 ≈ 0.0175, so every derivative drags a π/180 along. Radians are the unit where the circle measures itself.
